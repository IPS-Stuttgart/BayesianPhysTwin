# Dynamic TAPNext++ Short-Sentinel V6 Source Protocol

## Status

This is a new post-open source-development arm defined after V5 failed its
frame-zero-to-frame-57 provider-support gate. It is not a continuation or
retuning of V5, a prospective target evaluation, a confirmation result, or a
state-of-the-art claim.

The already-open source case may be used for provider and disjoint
hidden-identity evaluation. The V1 sealed target cohort, fresh-object locks,
and all held-v8 artifacts remain outside the information boundary.

## Frozen Hypothesis

V5 had ample target-free graph geometry but lost every identity at its
frame-57 multiview gate. V6 tests whether the same common-bias sentinel
mechanism becomes observable when the carrier begins at frame 51.

The fixed query schedule is:

- birth frame: 51;
- update frame: 57;
- 9 active graph identities;
- 3 physically low-motion sentinel identities;
- active displacement at least 2 mm over frames 51--57;
- sentinel displacement at most 0.5 mm over frames 51--57;
- at least three predicted visible cameras per identity;
- one target-free eight-camera panel;
- no query reseeding or transfer of unused budget between roles.

These values were frozen before running TAPNext++ or reading a hidden-future
outcome. A target-free audit of the selected physical source rollout found 320
eligible active nodes and 122 eligible sentinel nodes at this interval.

## Provider and Debias Contract

TAPNext++ starts a fresh recurrent state at frame 51 and consumes frames
51--57. Query association uses only frame-51 geometry, depth, mask, and camera
calibration. The loader may decode the complete permitted prefix for the
existing camera certificate, but frames before 51 do not enter the tracker
state.

All three sentinel identities must have accepted birth and frame-57 support.
Their displacement residual is:

```text
(observed frame-57 position - observed frame-51 position)
-
(physical frame-57 position - physical frame-51 position)
```

Because all sentinels share one tracker and camera panel, they are treated as
one unknown-correlation group. The frozen four-sigma coherence test and
covariance-intersection treatment remain unchanged from V5.

An admitted common bias is subtracted from active displacement observations,
and its complete covariance is added to every active covariance. Sentinel
identities are never assimilated as state measurements. Any schedule,
association, support, coherence, selector, or pairwise-consensus failure
produces bit-exact persistence after frame 57.

## Source Execution Order

1. Build and seal the target-free schedule.
2. Run TAPNext++ and multiview fusion through frame 57.
3. Check endpoint support and sentinel-bias admission without reading hidden
   future identities.
4. Stop if the candidate is exact persistence.
5. Otherwise freeze the provider artifact.
6. Score only future identities disjoint from all 12 query identities.

The first smoke case is the same already-open shoe source case used by V3--V5.
V5 did not read its hidden-future outcome, so no V6 prediction or metric has
been selected using that outcome.

## Advancement Gate

V6 advances to a frozen opened-source transfer cohort only if the smoke case:

1. fills the complete 9-active/3-sentinel schedule;
2. supports all three sentinels at birth and update;
3. admits a coherent bias estimate;
4. supplies enough active support for the unchanged nine-inlier pairwise gate;
5. improves disjoint hidden-identity RMSE over exact persistence;
6. improves disjoint hidden Chamfer over exact persistence;
7. improves both metrics over the selected physical-or-persistence backbone.

A tie fails. Any hidden-outcome failure closes this exact arm. No query birth,
motion threshold, camera count, support requirement, covariance rule, or
assimilation hyperparameter may be changed afterward on this source case.

A smoke pass permits only an opened-source transfer run. An independent
fresh-object evaluation requires a new preregistration, object-hash exclusion
manifest, immutable prediction barrier, and a cohort disjoint from all prior
development objects.
