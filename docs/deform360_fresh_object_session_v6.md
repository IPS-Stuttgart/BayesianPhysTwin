# Deform360 fresh object-session prospective v6

## Purpose

Version 6 is a separately versioned prospective study. It does not edit,
reinterpret, or rescue Deform360 v5. V5 keeps its own ten development objects,
twelve confirmation objects, source gate, and terminal result. The v6 design
and source-only selection are frozen before reading a v5 terminal outcome.
V6 can open no target payload until v5 has an immutable terminal record.

The v6 question is narrower and harder:

> Does one challenger selected entirely on opened source object-session units,
> with its covariance method, guard, and interval calibration frozen on source,
> improve future physical-query prediction on sixteen previously untouched
> Deform360 object-session units over both the physical fallback and the last
> causal residual?

The machine-readable policy is
`protocols/locks/deform360_official_hub_fresh_object_session_v6.json`.

## Relationship to v5

V6 is not a second chance chosen after seeing v5. The candidate roster, source
selection, covariance selection, and guard calibration are sealed before the
v5 terminal outcome is read. If the v6 source gate passes, continuation to the
fresh target cohort is precommitted and does not depend on whether v5 is
positive or negative.

Both terminal records must be reported together. A positive v6 result does not
override or relabel a negative v5 result. A cross-version replication claim is
permitted only when both v5 and v6 are positive under their own immutable
locks. A standalone v6 claim remains limited to the sixteen fresh v6 units.

## Why v6 is scientifically distinct

V5 fixes one joint-sparse visuotactile candidate on one locked twelve-object
confirmation cohort. V6 changes neither that candidate nor that cohort. Instead,
it introduces three independent safeguards before a new target cohort exists:

1. a nested source-only tournament may advance at most one challenger;
2. a nested source-only covariance portfolio chooses one query-covariance
   interpretation and calibrates its variance and interval; and
3. a nested source-only guard freezes one tie-preserving acceptance threshold.

The candidate roster is frozen at the v6 design revision. It contains the
merged dynamic endpoint model-average v2 and the merged v5 joint-sparse
visuotactile method. The registered reference is the last causal residual. If
the reference remains selected, or if the challenger, covariance, or guard is
unstable under outer leave-one-object-session-out evaluation, v6 terminates as
a source-negative result without selecting or opening target data.

## Fresh object-session cohort

V6 uses sixteen unique physical-object-session units: eight sheet and eight
volumetric. Every object and selected episode must be untouched by every prior
opened, reserved, cache-touched, source-target, or v5 development/confirmation
boundary. V5 confirmation objects are excluded even if they remain sealed.

Selection uses only official raw object-directory names, selected
`metadata.json` files, and the frozen exclusion identities. Objects and episodes
are ranked by separate SHA-256 salts. Camera, depth, geometry, tactile, robot,
prediction, residual, future-frame, and outcome values are forbidden until a
content-addressed selection lock is committed. Insufficient eligible units is a
complete support-negative result; no object or episode is replaced.

This route uses released real-world Deform360 recordings and the frozen official
processing revision. It requires no new recording, robot execution, or human
scientific approval.

## Source-only challenger selection

The ten v5 development object-session units are source units only. V6 requires
all candidate predictions and causal-prefix risk scores to be sealed before any
source suffix score is attached. The existing candidate-tournament contract is
then run in ten outer folds with nine training units per fold.

The reference is `B1_last_causal_residual`. The two frozen challengers are:

- `D1_dynamic_endpoint_model_average_v2`; and
- `VT1_joint_sparse_visuotactile_guarded_v5`.

The tournament may advance exactly zero or one challenger. It uses equal source
unit weighting, point loss, Gaussian proper score, full interval width,
harmful-update control, exact point-and-interval fallback, and deterministic
complexity tie-breaking. A challenger must be the outer-fold winner in at least
eight of ten folds, avoid regression on at least eight of ten held-out units and
four of five per stratum, and improve point loss over the reference by at least
two percent. Otherwise the reference is retained and v6 stops before target
selection.

## Guard calibration

Each challenger receives a candidate-specific guard inside every outer source
fold. Candidate prediction and risk score are sealed before suffix scoring. The
guard uses the fixed semantics `lower-is-safer-inclusive-threshold-v1` and
accepts complete tied score blocks; object identities cannot split a tie.

A source guard must accept at least eight of ten units and at least four of five
per stratum, with at most one harmful source unit under the frozen two-percent
margin. Only after cross-fitted candidate, covariance, and guard selection may
one full-source refit be produced. That refit changes no candidate, covariance
method, feature, threshold rule, or decision rule. Target adaptation is
forbidden, and every rejection returns the exact fallback point and interval.

## Covariance and interval calibration

For the selected challenger, v6 retains the complete common-query covariance
portfolio:

- working IRLS;
- observed information;
- group sandwich; and
- exact prior fallback only for rejected inference.

Every available method must be present, or carry an explicit source-owned
unavailability reason. Covariance selection is nested inside the candidate
outer folds. It uses proper score, object-session coverage, full interval width,
and the deterministic complexity order shown above. The same method must win at
least eight of ten outer folds.

Raw covariance is never called calibrated. A source-only variance scale and
90-percent grouped split-conformal interval are fitted after method selection.
Cross-fitted source coverage must lie between 80 and 98 percent, proper score
must not regress against the calibrated last-residual reference, and mean full
interval width may not exceed 1.25 times the reference. Confirmation-side
adaptation is forbidden.

## Causal boundary and endpoint

The registered action window remains `[0, 81)`. Candidate predictions, factors,
risk scores, and acceptance decisions may use only `[0, 58)`. Frames `[58, 76)`
form the future evaluation interval, and `[76, 81)` is an unscored buffer.

Two endpoint views per object-session are reserved by a metadata-only SHA-256
rule before target payload opening. Reserved views contribute no likelihood.
The primary endpoint is future held-out-view geometry error under the frozen
official processing revision. Results are averaged within object-session first;
all sixteen units then receive equal weight.

## Target methods

Only four methods are evaluated on the fresh cohort:

| ID | Role |
| --- | --- |
| `B0_physical_fallback` | unchanged baseline and exact fallback |
| `B1_last_causal_residual` | registered simple reference |
| `C1_source_selected_challenger_guarded` | sole primary candidate |
| `C2_source_selected_challenger_unguarded` | safety diagnostic |

All covariance-portfolio members are reported diagnostically, but only the
source-selected covariance is claim-bearing. No second challenger can be
promoted after target opening. Causal4D is evaluated only after a positive v6
BayesianPhysTwin result.

## Positive decision

The guarded selected challenger is positive only if every frozen condition
passes:

1. at least 10 percent lower primary loss than physical fallback;
2. at least 5 percent lower primary loss than last residual;
3. at least 2 percent lower Gaussian NLL than calibrated last residual;
4. family-wise 95 percent paired-bootstrap evidence for all three confirmatory
   contrasts: primary loss versus physical fallback, primary loss versus last
   residual, and Gaussian NLL versus last residual;
5. each contrast uses a 98.333-percent upper bound under Bonferroni control;
6. at least 13 of 16 units improve against each point-loss comparator;
7. at least 6 of 8 units improve in each stratum against each comparator;
8. at least 12 of 16 units and 6 of 8 per stratum are accepted;
9. no accepted unit exceeds physical fallback by more than 2 percent;
10. neither stratum mean regresses by more than 2 percent;
11. worst-unit regression is at most 10 percent;
12. object-session-balanced 90 percent coverage lies between 80 and 98 percent;
13. mean full interval width is at most 1.25 times the calibrated reference;
    and
14. every rejection reproduces the physical fallback point prediction and full
    interval exactly.

Requiring at least 13 improvements among 16 units has one-sided sign
probability `697 / 65536`, about 0.0106, under a 50/50 null. This is supporting
count evidence; the paired bootstrap is the registered effect-size interval.

A negative result is complete. Secondary endpoints and negative controls can
localize failure but cannot rescue the primary decision. No camera, object,
session, covariance method, candidate, threshold, or interval may be changed
after target access.

## Stage order

```text
freeze v6 before reading a v5 terminal outcome
-> seal all source candidate predictions and risk scores
-> run nested source candidate, covariance, and guard selection
-> stop source-negative if no stable challenger exists
-> complete or close v5 under its own immutable lock
-> continue v6 regardless of the sign of the v5 result
-> publish the disjoint metadata-only v6 selection lock
-> freeze exact execution revisions and machine authorization
-> open sixteen fresh object-session payloads once
-> jointly report v5 and v6 without relabeling either result
-> publish a positive or negative v6 object-session result
```

## Claim boundary

A positive result supports only the selected challenger on the sixteen locked
fresh Deform360 object-session units. It does not certify deployment safety,
small population harm probability, performance outside this cohort, Causal4D
intervention benefit, benchmark parity, or state of the art.
