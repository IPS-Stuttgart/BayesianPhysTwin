# Robust and open-set interventional cause attribution

## Why this extension is needed

The nominal certificate in `interventional_cause_identifiability_v1` treats the
registered response signatures as exact. On real data, every state, material,
contact, bias, and discrepancy signature is estimated. A second failure mode is
more fundamental: a finite registered cause family can force a plausible label
even when the true mechanism is absent from that family.

This extension adds two fail-closed outcomes:

1. `identifiable_but_unstable` when nominal separation is smaller than the
   declared signature and observation uncertainty; and
2. `unregistered_cause` when no mixture of registered causes and nuisance can
   explain the stacked intervention response within the declared uncertainty.

## Robust query theorem

For a target cause `c`, let the nominal competing design be

\[
\widehat C_c=[\widehat N,\widehat S_1,\ldots,
\widehat S_{c-1},\widehat S_{c+1},\ldots]
\]

and let

\[
P_c^\perp=I-\widehat C_c\widehat C_c^\dagger,
\qquad
\widehat A_c=P_c^\perp\widehat S_c.
\]

When the registered query satisfies `B_c=M_c A_c`, estimate it by

\[
\widehat q_c=M_cP_c^\perp r.
\]

Assume blockwise deterministic bounds

\[
\|E_{j,u}\|_2\le\epsilon_{j,u},\quad
\|\beta_j\|_2\le R_j,\quad
\|e_u\|_2\le\rho_u,
\]

with analogous nuisance bounds. For intervention set `I`, vertical stacking gives

\[
\|E_{j,I}\|_2\le
\left(\sum_{u\in I}\epsilon_{j,u}^2\right)^{1/2}.
\]

Therefore

\[
\|\widehat q_c-q_c\|_2
\le
\|M_c\|_2
\left[
\left(\sum_{u\in I}\rho_u^2\right)^{1/2}
+R_N\left(\sum_{u\in I}\epsilon_{N,u}^2\right)^{1/2}
+\sum_j R_j\left(\sum_{u\in I}\epsilon_{j,u}^2\right)^{1/2}
\right].
\]

The proof is a projection and triangle-inequality argument: nominal competitors
are annihilated by `P_c^perp`; the remaining signature errors and observation
noise are nonexpansively projected and then amplified by at most `||M_c||_2`.

A cause query is promoted only when this bound is below its frozen task tolerance.
The smallest and cheapest sufficient intervention sets depend only on frozen
signatures and uncertainty budgets; no observed target residual is read during
intervention design.

## Open-set closure theorem

Let

\[
\widehat D=[\widehat N,\widehat S_1,\ldots,\widehat S_C],
\qquad P_D^\perp=I-\widehat D\widehat D^\dagger.
\]

Every response generated by the registered family under the same uncertainty
budgets must satisfy

\[
\|P_D^\perp r\|_2\le\rho_{\rm eff}.
\]

If the measured closure residual exceeds that radius, the complete registered
family is falsified and the only permitted attribution is `unregistered_cause`.
The converse is intentionally not claimed: an omitted mechanism inside the span
of the registered family is observationally indistinguishable without another
intervention or sensor.

## Paper-level consequence

The stronger scientific message is:

> Changed interventions do not merely improve cause classification. They make
> physical attribution falsifiable under estimated response models, and allow the
> twin to say that none of its registered explanations is adequate.

The controlled study includes exact-signature uncertainty, an omitted-cause
population, a wrong-action placebo, and a near-confounding stress where a nominal
point label is available but robust promotion is forbidden.
